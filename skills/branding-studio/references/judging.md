# Judging — compare before you conclude

Load this reference whenever creative work is compared, ranked or declared good enough: choosing between lineages (DIVERGE), committing a direction (COMMIT DIRECTION), testing applications (TEST IN USE) and closing the package (PACKAGE). It owns the format of every creative comparison; `identity-craft.md` owns what is looked at, `creative-direction.md` owns the gates a lineage must pass.

Without a format, a model invents the most available one: a rubric with numbers. A number for creative quality measures nothing and becomes the goal within one message. The rules below exist to make that impossible, not to make judgment slower.

## 1. Criteria exist before the work

Every comparison is judged against the **direction criteria** written in FRAME and sharpened in COMMIT DIRECTION (`SKILL.md`): three to five perceptions the work must produce and two or three it must avoid, each derived from the brand job, the position and, after commitment, the signature. A perception is written so that a reader could confirm or deny it from the artifact alone ("whoever sees this understands that a named person answers", "with the mark covered, the piece still reads as this brand", "it does not read as a bank or an app").

Criteria are never written after seeing the candidates, never adjusted to rescue one, and never borrowed from another brand's idea. If a comparison needs a criterion the list does not contain, that is a FRAME or direction problem; resolve it there, then compare again.

## 2. The comparison set

A candidate is judged beside two things it must beat, never alone:

- the **floor** — the competent-but-generic solution to this brief, produced deliberately as what category autocomplete would give (`creative-direction.md`, §3);
- the **bar** — two or three real identities from the brand's category and adjacent categories *in the brand's own market*, inspectable now, that the brand's audience actually meets. Never global icons or canonical logos: beside them a new mark measures familiarity, not quality, and a brief that admires one of them smuggles its idea into the criteria.

A candidate that does not beat the floor has failed, however polished. Beating the floor is the minimum; holding beside the bar is the target.

## 3. The format

- **Pairwise, blind.** Candidates carry neutral labels (A, B), no authorship, no maker's rationale, no history of earlier rounds. The judge receives the renders at the scale the reader will meet them, the criteria and nothing else.
- **Both orders.** Each pair is judged twice with the order swapped; a verdict that flips with the order is not a verdict.
- **Criterion by criterion.** For each criterion: which candidate produces it more clearly, and what in the artifact shows it. Then the weakest visible element of the preferred candidate and the defects, each located (a crop, a letter, an edge, a shadow).
- **No numbers.** No scores, no decimals, no averages, no rankings presented as measurement. Order is allowed; magnitude is not.
- **Stop on information, not on count.** Judge again only after a change that could alter the verdict; a round that changes nothing means the criteria or the direction are the problem (`SKILL.md`, Stage exits).

## 4. Who judges, and what that is worth

In decreasing order of strength:

1. **An isolated context** that has not seen the making: a subagent or a fresh session given only the renders and the criteria.
2. **The brand's audience**, through `audience-test.md`, for perception criteria only.
3. **The owner**, blind between candidate and floor, for perception and recognition criteria only ("which of these could belong to any company in your sector?", "is this you?"); never for craft, never asked which one they like.
4. **The maker**, in a cold pass: new message, renders and criteria only, defects named before merits. The maker ranks its own work leniently and prefers what resembles its own output, so a verdict reached this way is reported as the maker's, and the delivery says so.

Use the strongest judge available. When an isolated context can be started, the maker's cold pass is not used for a direction commitment or a package close.

Judging that two artifacts agree with each other, or that several same-model judges agree, is not evidence about an audience.

## 5. What a verdict may change

A judgment refines the artifact it judged. It does not reopen a direction, a master or a grammar that has already been proved and propagated: only failure in use, or the owner's authority, reopens those (`SKILL.md`, REFINE OR RE-DIVERGE). When repeated judgments keep pulling toward a route the criteria do not describe, the criteria or the direction are wrong, not the artifact; go upstream, with the owner in the loop.

A judgment is also not a generation brief. Mechanisms the judge admired in the bar or elsewhere do not become prompts; `visual-production.md` says what a generation request may contain.
