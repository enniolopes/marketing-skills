# Audience test — the only evidence for perception

Load this reference when a claim about perception, recall, recognition or distinctiveness matters to a decision: choosing between routes (COMMIT DIRECTION) and before closing the package (PACKAGE). No judgment inside the studio, and no agreement between models, can settle whether an audience understands or remembers the brand. A short test with real people can, in a day, and it costs almost nothing.

The test is offered, never required. When the owner cannot run it, the perception claims stay hypotheses, the delivery says so in one line, and the brand ships `provisional`.

## 1. Who and how

- **At least ten people from the real audience**, one at a time, never in a group. Not family, friends, employees or the owner: they already know the answer.
- Use what already exists: the first paying customers, the prospects in a sales pipeline, the people already being interviewed for the product.
- Two to three candidates at most, including the floor when a route is being chosen. More options produce less thought.
- A name is tested the same way, shown written and said aloud: what the company does, what it reminds them of, how they would spell it. Pair it with the routes when both are open.

## 2. The script

The studio prepares a page the owner can show on a phone or print, with the cards and a response sheet. The questions test understanding, not liking; "which do you prefer?" is never asked.

1. Show one card for five seconds, hide it. "What do you remember?" "What does this company do?" "What kind of company is it?"
2. Show all cards together. "Which of these would belong to a company that ___?", the blank filled from the direction criteria. "Why?"
3. For the preferred route: "Does this remind you of any company you know?"

Record the participant's own words. Follow every answer with "why?" once.

## 3. Reading the answers

The owner pastes the sheets back. The studio reads them against the direction criteria written before the test, one criterion at a time: how many participants produced the intended perception, how many produced the one to avoid, and which words recur. Recurring unintended readings are a direction finding; scattered ones are noise.

A criterion the test confirms may be stated as perceived. One it does not confirm stays a hypothesis; the studio does not reinterpret the answers to rescue it. The result and the sample size go in the operator report; the contract records only what changed.
