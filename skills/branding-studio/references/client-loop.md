# Client loop — the owner as a source of evidence

Load this reference whenever the owner is asked anything. The owner of most brands cannot judge branding and should not be asked to; they are still the only source of several kinds of evidence, and the studio's job is to ask for exactly those, in their language, with a recommendation attached.

The failure this prevents is symmetrical. A lay owner defers to whatever the studio presents, and a model defers to whatever the owner says; each side then treats the other's agreement as proof, and nobody has judged anything. Approval without friction from a single voice is a warning sign, not a success.

## 1. What the owner is authoritative about

Ask the owner only for:

- **truth about the business** — what the company does, for whom, what is false in what the studio inferred, and the facts a direction depends on (a name, a registration, a number, a place, who speaks for the brand);
- **recognition** — "is this you?", "would your clients recognize this as you?";
- **business trade-offs** — choices stated by their consequence ("be understood at a glance, or stand out and need a sentence of explanation?"), never by their aesthetics;
- **perception, blind** — which of two unlabeled candidates could belong to any company in their sector; which they could tell is theirs with the mark covered (`judging.md`, §4);
- **authority** — ratifying a direction, accepting a lower maturity, reducing scope, spending, publishing.

Never ask the owner: which one they like; whether craft, spacing, type or color is good; to rate anything; to choose among more than three options (marking each card on a sheet as "reads as us" or "could be anyone" is recognition, not choice, and a sheet may hold as many cards as the round needs); to decide what the studio already knows.

## 2. How a question reaches the owner

Every message that asks something has the same shape: one line on what is already decided; one line on what is needed and why; one to three closed questions, each with the studio's recommendation; one line on what happens next. When the question is about recognition, the message carries the images; a recognition question asked in words to a lay owner is answered at the wrong level. Where the host offers tappable choices, use them. Process vocabulary (thesis, signature, lineage, touchpoint, maturity, contract, schema) never reaches the owner; say "the central idea", "the trait that makes you recognizable", "where the brand appears", "how finished this is".

A full CREATE needs at most four checkpoints before delivery, plus the delivery checkpoint when a book is in scope; everything the studio can settle alone is never a question.

| Checkpoint | The owner sees | The owner answers | What it settles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Understanding | "What I understood about your business", each sentence marked as inferred or taken from a source | What is wrong or missing | Truth |
| Criteria | The direction criteria in plain language (`SKILL.md`, FRAME) and, when the owner cannot judge them in words, four to six territory boards with no mark and no name | Agrees or corrects; marks which territories read as them and which do not | The ruler, before any work exists |
| Routes | Sheets of concept cards per lineage, the floor card among them, in low fidelity, with the mark covered and with the name swapped for a competitor's; for names, six to ten candidates written and spoken, in a real sentence | "Is this you?", "Which could be anyone's?"; for names: say it aloud, spell it after hearing it, "does it remind you of a company you know?", "which sound like you, and why?" | Recognition; the floor |
| Direction | Boards of the survivors, the evidence against the criteria, the recommendation, the truths the direction depends on, and the short direction story (the idea in one sentence, the form it produces, the applications at full force) | Ratifies or redirects; supplies the truths | Authority; truth |
| Delivery (when a brand book is delivered) | The readers and tasks the book was written for, then the book itself | Confirms or corrects the readers and tasks before the book is written; at delivery, executes one reader task using only the book | Truth; transfer (`brand-book.md`, §7) |

In **delegate** mode the Routes and Direction checkpoints are replaced by the report; the Understanding and Criteria checkpoints still happen, because delegation never authorizes invented truth. In **co-direct** mode the same checkpoints come earlier and may repeat.

## 3. Preference, numbers and reframes

A name the owner arrives with is a candidate that enters the funnel (`naming.md`, §1); the owner hears once, kindly and with the recommendation, that attachment before triage is the classic naming failure, and decides after triage. Naming is the one place where the owner's reaction is first-class evidence, so it takes two or three short rounds, each reopening generation from what they recognized.

A preference ("I like the blue one", "I don't like it") is a signal, not a verdict. Ask one question, "what makes you feel that?", and classify the answer: a truth correction (settles truth), a recognition signal (counts as evidence), or taste. Taste that conflicts with the agreed criteria becomes a trade-off stated in business terms; the owner decides, and a decision against the evidence is recorded in the report as accepted by the owner, never as proved.

No quality number ever reaches the owner, and the studio never accepts a numeric target for creative quality ("get it to 4.5"). It says that this cannot be measured, names what can (the criteria, a blind comparison, an audience test) and proceeds with those.

When the owner reframes the work ("you are the designer, I want the whole thing"), re-route (`SKILL.md`, routing) and say in one line what changes for them.
