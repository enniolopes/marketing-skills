# Naming — a funnel with the owner inside it

Treat naming as a strategic, linguistic and legal-risk decision, and as the decision with the widest fan-out in the brand: every mark, domain, filing and document inherits it. Name before logo. Triage before attachment.

## 1. When the name is open

The name is in scope when the owner offers one tentatively ("something like X"), asks for alternatives, has none, or when triage of a given name finds a close collision, a homophone in the category or a word so generic in the market that search returns addresses instead of the brand. A name the owner brings is a candidate, not a decision: it enters the funnel below and passes the same filters, and the owner hears once, with the recommendation, that attachment before triage is the classic naming failure. After that it is their call.

Naming starts after FRAME. A name generated before the brand job and position are agreed is a name for a brand that does not exist yet.

## 2. Criteria and the map

Write the naming criteria before any candidate exists, as part of the direction criteria (`SKILL.md`, FRAME): what the name must signal or deliberately leave open; markets and languages; how much category legibility is needed now; future scope tolerance; architecture or endorsement relationship; phonetic constraints; relevant legal classes when triage is needed; portfolio patterns to avoid or share.

Then map the category's names on two axes, expected/unexpected and descriptive/abstract, and place the competitors on it. The map shows the floor (the name category autocomplete would give, including the suffix fads of the moment) and makes the chosen territory a decision the owner can confirm in business terms: fit in and read as established, or stand out and need a sentence of explanation. Record the territory in the criteria.

## 3. Generate wide, with a bare request

Volume is cheap and convergence is the enemy: a model asked for names returns the same twenty names everyone gets. Counter it structurally, never with "be creative":

- generate the floor first and exclude it;
- generate by lineage, with a quota per lineage, each lineage starting from a different source domain: descriptive, suggestive, metaphorical, real word, compound, coined, morphological, initials where context genuinely supports them, and the name of a person or place (the standard answer for professional services, with the warning that compound names collapse into initials);
- when coverage is narrow, open a remote seed and bridge it back to true brand material (`creative-direction.md`, §4);
- aim for the order of a hundred candidates before any filter; a hundred names cost less than one wrong one.

The generation request itself is short and unformatted: the funnel decides when, how many and from what; it does not shape the words. No example names, no designers, no brands "to sound like" inside the request. Labels help compare candidates; they are not rankings, and every cell does not need a candidate.

## 4. Filter in stages, cheapest first

Cost rises down the funnel, so each stage removes what the next, dearer stage should never see:

1. **Criteria and the map**: down to fifteen or twenty.
2. **Cheap triage on all of them**, before any card exists: web search for the name beside the category and the market, domains and handles, homophones and near-spellings in the category and the owner's channels, the word's meaning and slang in the declared languages, how it reads written and spoken. Report each collision by **proximity**: same category, same market, same channel, confusable aloud. Only close proximity blocks; a different category or region the brand will never trade in usually does not.
3. **Speech and language** on what survives (§5): down to six or ten.
4. **The owner, round one** (`client-loop.md`): which sound like them and why; down to three.
5. **Deep triage on the three**: current authoritative sources for procedures, current search systems for marks in the relevant classes, confusingly similar marks and not only exact strings. Verdict per name: no apparent collision found in triage; apparent collision or risk, with its proximity; uncertain, specialist review needed. Model or web triage is never definitive legal clearance.

Two or three rounds with the owner are normal: each round reopens generation from what they recognized, not from what they liked.

## 5. Test speech and language

For every declared market and language: pronunciation after reading, spelling after hearing, the telephone test (say it to a customer, to a printer, to a bank; would anyone feel foolish?), how it looks written and in a URL, ambiguity, unintended slang or connotation, cross-language risk, and similarity to confusing category or portfolio names. Model judgment is triage; require native review when material uncertainty remains.

## 6. Converge against criteria, not taste

Judge survivors in the format of `judging.md`, blind, against the floor and the criteria, on: fit to the naming job and territory; distinctiveness in context, with the swap test as the test of appropriateness (a name that would work as well for another company is not this company's name); brevity, including resistance to becoming a nickname or initials; imagery (concrete words and proper names are recalled more than Latinate roots); spelling and pronunciation; likability in use; extendibility into the identity and into future scope; language robustness; legal and domain risk; creative potential inside the system. A name's visual hook (a letter that invites a symbol) is a property to note, never the brand idea.

Recommend one name; present alternatives only when they materially improve the decision or the owner asks. Perception claims (memorable, distinctive to the audience) are settled by `audience-test.md`, with the name shown written and spoken.

## 7. Persist the decision, hand over the actions

When naming belongs in the contract: the selected name, a concise naming job when it prevents drift, meaningful exclusions, markets that materially constrain use, and dated clearance and linguistic triage with any unresolved specialist dependency. Not the candidate list.

The operator report lists what only the owner can do, with what triage found: register the domains and handles, file the mark in the relevant classes, obtain specialist review where the consequence requires it. For a market-facing new name, an unresolved collision or missing high-consequence legal review stays an explicit dependency in the delivery report, and the brand ships `provisional` until it is resolved.
